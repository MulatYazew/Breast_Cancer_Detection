# BI-RADS ultrasound lexicon (5th edition) — knowledge base for Stage 6

Paraphrased summaries of the ACR BI-RADS Atlas 5th Edition ultrasound lexicon
terms (see the ACR quick-reference card; full Atlas at acr.org/birads). Each
`## [id]` heading is one retrievable chunk; the id is what reports cite.

## [us-shape-oval] Mass shape: oval
An oval mass is elliptical or egg-shaped; it may show two or three gentle
undulations (macrolobulation). Oval shape favors a benign process, for example a
fibroadenoma, when the other features are also benign.

## [us-shape-round] Mass shape: round
A round mass is spherical, ball-shaped, or circular; its anteroposterior and
transverse diameters are about equal. Round shape alone is not suspicious, but it
carries less benign weight than oval.

## [us-shape-irregular] Mass shape: irregular
An irregular mass is neither round nor oval. Irregular shape is a suspicious
feature and, on its own, usually moves the assessment into category 4 or higher.

## [us-orientation-parallel] Orientation: parallel
Parallel orientation means the long axis of the mass is parallel to the skin
("wider than tall"). This is typical of benign masses such as fibroadenomas,
though it doesn't exclude malignancy.

## [us-orientation-not-parallel] Orientation: not parallel
Not-parallel orientation means the long axis is not parallel to the skin, so the
anteroposterior dimension exceeds the transverse one ("taller than wide").
This is a suspicious feature, associated with growth across tissue planes.

## [us-margin-circumscribed] Margin: circumscribed
A circumscribed margin is well defined, with an abrupt transition between the
lesion and the surrounding tissue along its entire border. Together with oval
shape and parallel orientation, it supports a benign or probably benign
assessment.

## [us-margin-not-circumscribed] Margin: not circumscribed
If any part of the margin is not circumscribed, the margin is classed as not
circumscribed and the most suspicious subtype is recorded: indistinct, angular,
microlobulated, or spiculated. Each subtype is a suspicious feature.

## [us-margin-indistinct] Margin: indistinct
Indistinct means there is no clear demarcation between all or part of the
margin and the surrounding tissue. It can reflect infiltration, and also an
echogenic rim.

## [us-margin-angular] Margin: angular
Angular means some or all of the margin has sharp corners, often forming acute
angles. This is a suspicious feature.

## [us-margin-microlobulated] Margin: microlobulated
Microlobulated means the margin has short-cycle undulations, giving a scalloped
appearance. This is a suspicious feature.

## [us-margin-spiculated] Margin: spiculated
Spiculated means sharp lines radiate from the margin. This is among the
features most predictive of malignancy.

## [us-echo-anechoic] Echo pattern: anechoic
Anechoic means no internal echoes. Together with a circumscribed margin and
posterior enhancement, it defines a simple cyst.

## [us-echo-hypoechoic] Echo pattern: hypoechoic
Hypoechoic means less echogenic than subcutaneous fat. Both benign and
malignant solid masses are commonly hypoechoic, so this pattern alone has
limited diagnostic specificity.

## [us-echo-isoechoic] Echo pattern: isoechoic
Isoechoic means the same echogenicity as subcutaneous fat. Isoechoic masses can
be hard to see. The pattern alone has limited specificity.

## [us-echo-hyperechoic] Echo pattern: hyperechoic
Hyperechoic means more echogenic than fat, equal to fibroglandular tissue.
Uniformly hyperechoic masses are usually benign (for example fat necrosis,
lipoma, or hamartoma).

## [us-echo-heterogeneous] Echo pattern: heterogeneous
A heterogeneous mass has a mixture of echogenic patterns within a solid mass.
It may be seen in both benign and malignant lesions. It is weakly informative
and is best interpreted together with shape and margin.

## [us-echo-complex] Echo pattern: complex cystic and solid
A complex cystic and solid mass contains both anechoic (cystic) and echogenic
(solid) components. This pattern warrants tissue diagnosis in most cases, so it
is a suspicious feature.

## [us-posterior-none] Posterior features: none
With no posterior features, there is neither enhancement nor shadowing deep to
the mass. This is common in both benign and malignant masses.

## [us-posterior-enhancement] Posterior features: enhancement
Posterior enhancement means increased echoes deep to the mass, because sound
passes through it easily. It is typical of cysts, but it also occurs in some
high-grade or mucinous cancers, so it is not reassuring on its own for solid
masses.

## [us-posterior-shadowing] Posterior features: shadowing
Posterior shadowing means attenuation (darkening) deep to the mass. It is a
suspicious feature, although postsurgical scarring, fibrosis, and some benign
lesions can also shadow.

## [us-posterior-combined] Posterior features: combined pattern
A combined pattern means more than one posterior pattern is present, for
example shadowing in one part and enhancement in another.

## [us-calcifications] Calcifications on ultrasound
Calcifications appear as echogenic foci within or outside a mass, or within
ducts. Ultrasound characterizes calcifications less reliably than mammography,
so correlate with mammography when calcifications are suspected.

## [us-not-assessable-bmode] Features not assessable from a single B-mode image
A single grayscale (B-mode) still image can't assess several lexicon
categories:
- vascularity (requires Doppler)
- elasticity (requires elastography)
- skin changes, edema, and duct changes (require the full field of view and
  real-time scanning)
- special cases (simple cyst confirmation, clustered microcysts, lymph nodes,
  implants, vascular abnormalities, postsurgical collections, fat necrosis),
  which depend on clinical history and real-time evaluation

A complete assessment needs these to be evaluated by the clinician.

## [us-special-simple-cyst] Special case: simple cyst
A simple cyst is circumscribed, round or oval, anechoic, and shows posterior
enhancement. It is benign (BI-RADS 2) and needs no further workup.

## [us-probably-benign-solid] Probably benign solid mass
A solid mass that is oval, parallel, and circumscribed, with no suspicious
features, is typically assessed as BI-RADS 3 (probably benign) on an initial
examination. The likelihood of malignancy is at most 2%, and short-interval
follow-up is advised.

## [us-suspicious-features] Suspicious ultrasound features
Each of the following features raises suspicion:
- irregular shape
- not-parallel orientation
- any non-circumscribed margin (indistinct, angular, microlobulated, or
  spiculated)
- posterior shadowing
- a complex cystic and solid echo pattern

A mass with any of these features is generally assessed as BI-RADS 4 or 5. The
more features present, the higher the subcategory.
