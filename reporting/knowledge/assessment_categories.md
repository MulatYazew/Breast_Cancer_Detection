# BI-RADS assessment categories (5th edition): likelihood of malignancy and management

## [cat-0] Category 0: Incomplete
Ultrasound: incomplete, needs additional imaging evaluation. Use this category
when the findings can't be fully characterized, for example when the lesion is
only partially imaged. Likelihood of malignancy: not applicable. Management:
recall for additional imaging.

## [cat-1] Category 1: Negative
There is nothing to comment on. Likelihood of malignancy is essentially 0%.
Management: routine screening. Not used when a mass is being described.

## [cat-2] Category 2: Benign
The finding is definitely benign, for example a simple cyst. Likelihood of
malignancy is essentially 0%. Management: routine screening.

## [cat-3] Category 3: Probably benign
Likelihood of malignancy is greater than 0% and at most 2%. Management:
short-interval follow-up, usually at 6 months, or continued surveillance. It is
reserved for findings with all benign features, such as an oval, parallel,
circumscribed solid mass on an initial exam.

## [cat-4a] Category 4A: Low suspicion for malignancy
Likelihood of malignancy is greater than 2% and at most 10%. Management: tissue
diagnosis (biopsy). A benign biopsy result is expected and is usually
concordant.

## [cat-4b] Category 4B: Moderate suspicion for malignancy
Likelihood of malignancy is greater than 10% and at most 50%. Management: tissue
diagnosis, with radiology–pathology correlation.

## [cat-4c] Category 4C: High suspicion for malignancy
Likelihood of malignancy is greater than 50% and less than 95%. Management:
tissue diagnosis. A benign result needs careful concordance review.

## [cat-5] Category 5: Highly suggestive of malignancy
Likelihood of malignancy is 95% or greater. Management: tissue diagnosis. A
benign biopsy result is considered discordant.

## [cat-6] Category 6: Known biopsy-proven malignancy
This category applies only after malignancy has been proven by biopsy, before
definitive therapy. Management: surgical excision when clinically appropriate.
It is never assigned from imaging features alone, and only the clinician can
assign it.
